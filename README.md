## 🌐 Project Overview

## Mission

Somatic Cognitive Function is a research repository dedicated to investigating the consequences of declining somatic awareness, diminished cognitive-body integration, and the gradual loss of conscious interaction with biological processes that continue operating automatically.

This project explores whether modern environments, conveniences, habits, and behavioral patterns contribute to a reduction in awareness of the body's physical and cognitive systems.

The repository investigates what may occur when awareness of posture, movement, breathing, sensory processing, coordination, and internal bodily function becomes progressively disconnected from conscious experience.

---

## Central Investigation

The primary question guiding this repository is:

> What are the long-term consequences of losing awareness of the processes that keep the body and mind functioning?

Many biological functions continue operating whether they are consciously observed or not.

Examples include:

- Breathing
- Walking
- Balance
- Posture
- Swallowing
- Sensory processing
- Visual processing
- Motor coordination

Because these processes are largely automatic, deficiencies may develop gradually without immediate recognition.

This repository investigates whether recurring physical, cognitive, sensory, respiratory, and postural limitations may emerge when attention and awareness are removed from those systems.

---

## Working Premise

A function does not need to stop working in order to become deficient.

A person may continue to:

- Walk
- Stand
- Breathe
- Think
- Speak
- Move

while remaining unaware of restrictions, asymmetries, compensations, inefficiencies, or reduced functionality that have become normalized over time.

The repository investigates whether awareness itself may be a contributing factor in maintaining, restoring, or improving function.

---

## Areas of Investigation

### Somatic Awareness

Investigation into the relationship between conscious awareness and physical function.

Questions include:

- Are certain body regions absent from conscious awareness?
- Are some regions easier to perceive than others?
- Can disconnected areas be reintegrated into awareness?

---

### Cognitive Awareness

Investigation into how attention is directed, maintained, and redistributed.

Questions include:

- What happens when consciousness repeatedly favors external processing over internal processing?
- Can awareness of internal processes be restored through deliberate practice?

---

### Respiratory Function

Investigation into breathing behavior, respiratory awareness, and respiratory coordination.

Questions include:

- Are all regions involved in breathing being utilized equally?
- Can respiratory awareness influence perception of other bodily systems?
- What occurs when breathing becomes predominantly automatic and unexamined?

---

### Motor Coordination

Investigation into automatic movement patterns.

Questions include:

- What happens when conscious control is introduced into automatic movements?
- Do unnoticed compensations become visible through directed awareness?
- Can movement patterns be retrained through observation and practice?

---

### Posture and Alignment

Investigation into relationships between posture, body mapping, movement, and awareness.

Questions include:

- Can awareness reveal postural deficiencies that are normally hidden?
- Can alignment improve when the body is examined as an integrated system rather than as isolated parts?

---

## Hypothesis Framework

Current hypotheses explore the possibility that:

- Awareness gaps contribute to physical deficiencies.
- Awareness gaps contribute to cognitive deficiencies.
- Breathing patterns influence attention allocation.
- Postural habits influence sensory and motor function.
- Automatic processes may conceal evolving limitations.
- Repeated observation and deliberate practice may restore awareness of overlooked systems.

These hypotheses remain under investigation and are not presented as established scientific conclusions.

---

## Why This Research Matters

If deficiencies can develop without immediate awareness, then individuals and populations may gradually adapt to limitations without recognizing they have occurred.

Under this framework, symptoms may not always be the first sign of decline.

Loss of awareness itself may be an early indicator.

Understanding how awareness, posture, movement, respiration, cognition, and perception interact could provide insight into how humans adapt, compensate, and potentially recover lost functionality.

---

## Repository Goal

The goal of this repository is to systematically document observations, hypotheses, experiments, and analyses relating to the relationship between:

- Somatic function
- Cognitive function
- Awareness
- Attention
- Movement
- Respiration
- Sensory perception
- Human adaptation

while investigating whether deliberate awareness can reveal, explain, or help address previously unnoticed physical and cognitive deficiencies.
