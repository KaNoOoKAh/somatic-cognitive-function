## Field Observations: Visual Tracking & Orbital Asymmetry

### 1. Intermittent Strabismus (Ocular Misalignment)
- **Observed Behavior:** Individuals turning corners show temporary loss of binocular alignment. One eye maintains structural fixation on the path forward, while the contralateral eye exhibits a distinct drift.
- **Biomechanical Mechanism:** Rapid focal adjustments from long-range navigation to immediate spatial obstacles strain the extraocular muscle pairs. Under cognitive load or fatigue, latent or intermittent misalignments (e.g., exotropia or esotropia) temporarily manifest.

### 2. Orbital Prominence Variations (Proptosis vs. Enophthalmos)
- **Observed Behavior:** Marked structural asymmetry in eye depth. Specific cases present with one eye noticeably protruding (exophthalmos) while the opposite eye appears deeply set within the orbital socket (enophthalmos).
- **Anatomical Drivers:** Variations are tied to individual orbital anatomy, axial length differences (high myopia causing physical elongation of the globe), or mechanical tissue discrepancies within the retrobulbar space. 

### 3. Combined Somatic Presentation
- **Interaction:** Mechanical strain from structural globe protrusion alters the resting tension of the surrounding extraocular muscles. This structural variance increases the likelihood of secondary tracking drift when the subject undergoes rapid shifts in visual focus.
