## 1. Objective

To document and analyze qualitative sensory feedback variations observed while introducing diverse acoustic frequencies, tones, and musical inputs to a garden environment, specifically measuring transmission differences between human tactile (hand) and cranial/auditory (left ear/lateral skull) contact with a tree.

## 2. Experimental Observations

### 2.1 Acoustic Inoculation

* **Input:** Varied music genres, specific localized tones, and targeted frequencies.
* **Medium:** Broadcasted via an external speaker placed within a garden ecosystem.

### 2.2 Tactile Reception (Hands)

* **Interface:** Manual palpation of the tree trunk with both hands.
* **Feedback:** Exceptional mechanical transmission. Vibrations and musical cadence were clearly perceptible through the dermal and musculoskeletal receptors of the hands.

### 2.3 Cranio-Auditory Reception (Left Ear / Lateral Cranium)

* **Interface:** Direct contact of the left pinna and the temporal/lateral region of the head against the tree surface.
* **Feedback:** Complete absence of mechanical or auditory feedback. No structural vibrations, resonance, or acoustic signals were perceived through this specific physiological interface.

---

## 3. Somatocognitive & Energetic Dynamics

### 3.1 Unconscious Biomechanical/Energetic Exertion

During cranio-auditory contact, a distinct, unconscious physiological phenomenon was detected:

* **Directionality:** An outward vector of force or concentrated energy was exerted from the interior cranial space toward the ear and lateral head area.
* **Polarity:** This state presented an exact structural opposite to the receptive, inward-drawing concentration profile experienced during hand-mediated palpation.

### 3.2 Dynamic Vector Switching

* **Observation:** The subject demonstrated a pronounced directional asymmetry in intentional control.
* **Result:** Transitioning from an *outward-directed force* profile to an *inward-receptive force* profile required significantly less cognitive friction than the inverse operation.

---

## 4. Preliminary Conclusions & Future Inquiries

The stark contrast between highly conductive manual reception and completely dampened cranial reception suggests either a highly localized mechanical dampening at the tree-cranial interface, or a cognitive/neurological blocking mechanism induced by the outward cognitive force profile. 

Future observations should seek to normalize the internal attention profile to determine if internal inward-reception unlocks cranial vibration sensitivity.
