Observation: Tooth Sensation, Pressure, and Suction Response

A decaying tooth has been producing recurring pain, tenderness, and localized sensitivity. The sensation is difficult to describe precisely but is often experienced as a buildup of pressure, static, energy, or a shock-like feeling concentrated around the affected tooth and surrounding gum tissue.

Observation

Throughout repeated self-observation sessions, I noticed that the affected area becomes increasingly sensitive over time. The region eventually reaches a point where even light contact produces an unusually intense response.

The sensation often feels as though something is accumulating within the area. Whether this represents pressure, inflammation, nerve activity, increased awareness, or another process remains unknown.

Suction Technique Observation

While in a relaxed state, I experimented with creating suction around the affected tooth using my tongue and inner cheek.

Each time the suction force was applied, I experienced a brief but distinct shock-like sensation that appeared to travel beyond the immediate area of the tooth. The sensation was strong enough to be noticeable throughout much of my body.

Immediately following this sensation:

The tenderness of the affected area decreased.
The tooth and surrounding gum felt less reactive.
The soreness temporarily diminished.

This effect was observed repeatedly.

Approximate repetitions: 20+

Observed outcome: Similar response during each attempt.

Over time, the tenderness gradually returned, at which point the process could be repeated with similar results.

Mechanical Pressure Observation

A second observation involved chewing on the wooden portion of a standard No. 2 pencil.

While applying pressure through controlled chewing, I noticed:

Reduced discomfort in the affected area.
Temporary relief of tenderness.
A change in the overall sensation surrounding the tooth.

The exact reason for this response is unknown.

Notes

At present, I cannot determine whether these effects are caused by:

Changes in pressure
Changes in muscular tension
Changes in nerve activity
Changes in blood flow
Increased awareness of the area
A combination of multiple factors

The only observation I can confidently make is that alterations in suction, pressure, and force application appear capable of changing how the affected tooth and gum area feel, at least temporarily. The sensation can be recreated by applying pressure/force/energy to the jaw, mouth, and teeth area.
