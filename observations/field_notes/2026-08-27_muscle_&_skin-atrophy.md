### Observation Details

### 1. Gait and Lower Extremities

* **Active Walking Window:** 9:24 AM – 9:28 AM
* **Focus & Concentration:** Movement requires highly conscious mental focus. The primary mental connection feels localized along a line from the eyes, down to the knees, and terminating at the back of the heel (specifically localized between the lower ankle and upper heel).
* **Weight Distribution:** Walking mechanics feel heavily dependent on the inner regions of the knees, the big toe, and the second toe.
* **Sensory Deficits:** 

  * The outer areas of the thighs feel heavy or devoid of tactile sensation.
  * Deep tissue regions within the center of the limbs/extremities exhibit a noticeable lack of feeling or a sense of dullness.
  * The limb feels like a single, unified mass rather than responsive, working muscle groups during live locomotion.

### 2. Rotational Mechanics & Posture

* **Initiating Turns:** Purposely initiating turns from the hips or legs feels superficial, as if the rotation is occurring strictly via the skin on the front of the leg or the front of the body.
* **Targeted Concentration:** Consciously shifting mental focus to less-stimulated regions—such as the back of the legs or the muscle tissue on the back—produces a completely different, un-stimulated sensory baseline.
* **Upper Body Interaction:** When walking with arms resting at the sides, the skin and muscle groups on the posterior (back) side of the body feel completely unstimulated and unused.

### 3. Fine Motor Skills & Tactile Grasp

* **Active Window:** 9:28 AM – 9:31 AM
* **Grip Mechanics:** When attempting to grasp or grip objects, the sensation is localized entirely to the inner skin layer of the hand. This remains true regardless of whether the hand is positioned open, palm up, or palm down.
* **Deep Sensation Deficit:** There is a distinct absence of deep sensation within the structural bones or the posterior skin of the hand. Grasping feels heavy, dull, and entirely dependent on conscious over-concentration due to generalized sensory numbness.

### 4. Oral and Facial Mechanics

* **Active Window:** 9:31 AM – 9:35 AM
* **Lip Connection:** Normal movement yields zero felt connection to the lips. Motor control feels distant, tracing back deeply to the posterior jawline or lightly through the interior cheeks.
* **Postural Corrections:** Consciously aligning the teeth/jaw and actively regulating lip positioning temporarily restores localized sensation.
* **Mastication & Speech:** Biting down shifts pressure almost exclusively to the lateral teeth on both the upper and lower jaws. Normal speech and tongue articulation occur with a complete absence of perceived physical force, mental pressure, or somatic connection to the tongue and the roof of the mouth.

### Environmental Notes

* **Observations during locomotion:** Logged while pacing back-and-forth to identify un-engaged muscle groups.
* **External Feedback:** Friends present (3 individuals) noted the activity, offering positive verbal encouragement ("good job and keep it moving").
